**Ontario insolvencies 2026** rose to **15,029 filings** in the second quarter, up **10.0% year over year** — more than three times the 3.0% increase recorded in Quebec over the same period. The Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) published the figures on 10 August, and the provincial detail shows that the national picture is not being felt evenly across the country.

![Ontario insolvencies 2026 — a household reviewing debt statements and bills at a kitchen table](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8478243/pexels-photo-8478243.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Summary

- **Ontario: 15,029 filings** in Q2 2026, **+10.0%** year over year — the highest volume of any province
- **Quebec: 9,361 filings**, **+3.0%** year over year
- National consumer insolvencies: **37,523**, **+6.9%** year over year
- Ontario is therefore running **above** the national rate of increase; Quebec well below it

## Ontario Insolvencies 2026: A Widening Provincial Gap

The two largest provinces account for a substantial share of all Canadian filings, and in this quarter they moved at markedly different speeds.

| Province | Q2 2026 filings | Year-over-year change |
|---|---|---|
| **Ontario** | **15,029** | **+10.0%** |
| **Quebec** | 9,361 | +3.0% |
| *National (consumer)* | *37,523* | *+6.9%* |

Ontario's rate of increase exceeded the national consumer figure by more than three percentage points. Quebec's was less than half the national rate. That divergence is the most interesting feature of the release, because the two provinces faced the same policy interest rate, the same federal benefit landscape and broadly the same inflation environment over the period.

## Ontario Insolvencies 2026: What the Data Does and Does Not Tell Us

An important caveat before drawing conclusions from the Ontario insolvencies 2026 release: the OSB reports **filings**, not causes. The statistics count how many Canadians entered a formal insolvency process and where they live. They do not explain why.

Commonly cited explanations for Ontario's faster increase centre on housing — higher average mortgage balances, the concentration of renewals coming off lower fixed rates, and rental costs in the Greater Toronto Area. Those explanations are plausible and widely discussed, but the OSB release does not itself establish them, and readers should treat them as context rather than confirmed findings.

What the data does establish is the divergence itself, and that it persisted while national filings were nearly flat quarter over quarter.

## Background: The National Picture

Nationally, Q2 2026 was the heaviest quarter for consumer insolvency filings since **2009**. The 37,523 consumer filings comprised 8,600 bankruptcies and 28,923 consumer proposals, and total insolvencies including business filings reached 39,104. Over the twelve months to 30 June, the OSB counted 145,762 consumer filings.

June's monthly figure of 13,254 filings was up 5.7% from May and 11.5% from a year earlier.

Charles St-Arnaud, chief economist at Servus Credit Union, told BNN Bloomberg that "high household debt and stagnating purchasing power have put pressure on Canadians' finances in recent years," while noting that insolvency rates "appear to be stabilizing near levels last seen in 2009." Our earlier coverage of [Q1 2026 insolvencies](/news/canada-consumer-insolvencies-q1-2026) set out the same trend one quarter earlier.

## Why This Matters for Ontario Households

The Ontario insolvencies 2026 count does not decide any individual application, and it is worth being clear about that. Lenders assess affordability — income, existing commitments, repayment history — not a postcode's filing rate.

Where the provincial angle genuinely matters is in the **rules and resources available locally**:

- **Ontario caps payday borrowing costs** in line with the federal ceiling of 14% of the amount advanced, with default interest limited to 2.5% per month and dishonoured-payment fees to $20.
- **A third payday loan within 63 days must be offered on an extended payment plan**, with instalments spread over multiple pay periods and no penalty for prepaying or exiting.
- **Collection conduct is regulated**: agencies must send written notice and wait six calendar days before contacting you, and may not contact you more than three times in seven days.
- **Free help exists province-wide** through Licensed Insolvency Trustees, non-profit credit counselling and community legal clinics.

## Ontario Insolvencies 2026: Key Takeaways

- Ontario recorded **15,029 insolvency filings** in Q2 2026, the most of any province
- Its **10.0%** annual increase was more than **triple Quebec's 3.0%**
- Ontario is running **above** the national consumer increase of 6.9%
- The OSB data reports filings, **not causes** — housing explanations are context, not findings
- Provincial rules, not provincial statistics, are what actually affect borrowers day to day

## Conclusion

The Ontario insolvencies 2026 figures show a province pulling away from the national trend rather than tracking it. With filings up 10% year over year against Quebec's 3%, the second quarter widened a gap that was already visible. For households in Ontario, the useful response is not to read a provincial number as a personal forecast, but to know that the province's own protections — cost caps, extended payment plans, collection limits and free trustee consultations — are available well before a filing becomes necessary.
