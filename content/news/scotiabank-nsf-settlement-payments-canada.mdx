**Scotiabank NSF settlement payments** started reaching customers in July 2026, and many are arriving without warning — a deposit of roughly **$42.82** landing in accounts belonging to people who never knew a lawsuit existed. The **$10.45 million** settlement was approved by the Ontario Superior Court of Justice on **June 12, 2026**, and resolves claims that the bank charged more than one NSF fee for what amounted to a single failed payment.

## What the Scotiabank NSF settlement payments are for

The class action behind the Scotiabank NSF settlement payments, brought by Toronto firm Koskie Minsky LLP, targeted a practice known as **duplicative NSF fees**.

The sequence is familiar to anyone who has run a thin balance. A biller presents a pre-authorized debit, the account is short, the payment fails, and the bank charges an NSF fee. The biller's system then automatically presents **the same payment again** days later. It fails again, and a second fee follows.

The claim argued that this amounted to penalising a customer repeatedly for one failed transaction, contrary to the account agreements and consumer protection law. **Scotiabank has not admitted liability and denies it.**

## 6 facts about the Scotiabank NSF settlement payments

1. **The fund is $10.45 million**, approved on **June 12, 2026**.
2. **Payments are roughly $42.82** per eligible class member.
3. **The class period is June 21, 2020 to April 30, 2024.**
4. **Eligibility is specific**: a **$48 NSF fee** charged on a re-presented pre-authorized debit **two to 30 days** after a previous fee, from the **same merchant**, for a **matching amount**.
5. **Notification came through Scotia Online and the mobile app**, from around **July 13, 2026** — not by letter.
6. **No claim form exists.** Scotiabank pays eligible accounts directly from its own records.

## Who qualifies for the Scotiabank NSF settlement payments

To receive one of the Scotiabank NSF settlement payments, class counsel's criteria require that you:

- Are a **Canadian resident**
- Hold a **Scotiabank personal deposit account**
- Have that account **open and able to accept deposits** at the distribution date
- Were charged the qualifying duplicate **$48 NSF fee** during the class period

The third condition is the one that quietly excludes people. Because the settlement pays by direct deposit into an account at the bank, a **closed account** is the single most common reason an otherwise eligible person receives nothing. Anyone in that position should contact class counsel rather than assume they have been left out.

## Why the amounts were so large in the first place

The refunds look modest against the fees that produced them.

Throughout the class period, NSF fees at Canada's six largest banks ran between **$45 and $48**, a weighted average of about **$46.85**. A duplicate charge therefore approached **$96** on a single failed payment — regardless of whether that payment was for $500 or $8. More than one in three Canadians incurs an NSF fee in a given year, and the cost falls hardest on people already living close to zero.

Federal policy has since changed that arithmetic. Since **March 12, 2026**, amendments to the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations cap NSF fees at **$10** at federally regulated banks. They also prohibit **more than one NSF fee within two business days** on the same account — the direct regulatory answer to the re-presentment problem this lawsuit was built on — and bar any fee when the overdraft is under **$10**.

A failed debit that cost about $48 in 2023, and could cost $96 if it bounced twice, now costs $10 once.

## Watch for the scam that follows every settlement

Publicity around the Scotiabank NSF settlement payments, like any automatic payout, reliably attracts fraud. In 2025 the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre logged more than **112,000 reports** and over **$704 million** in reported losses — a record — and settlement-themed approaches are a recurring theme.

Three rules cover it:

- **You never pay to receive a class action settlement.** No filing fee, no processing fee, no "release" fee.
- **Class counsel will not ask for your online banking password.** Scotiabank already has your account details; that is how the payment works.
- **Verify through the law firm's own site or the bank's app**, not through a link in a text or email.
