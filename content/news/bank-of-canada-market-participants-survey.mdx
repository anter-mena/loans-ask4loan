The **Bank of Canada market participants survey** for the second quarter of 2026 delivered a signal borrowers have not seen in two years: the median respondent now expects the **next move in the policy rate to be an increase, not a cut**. Financial market professionals surveyed between **June 11 and 18, 2026** expect the rate to hold at **2.25%** through every remaining 2026 decision, then rise to **2.50% in March 2027** and **2.75% by the third quarter of 2027**.

*Reported August 2, 2026, based on the Bank of Canada's Market Participants Survey for the second quarter of 2026. The survey records the views of about 26 financial market participants and is not a Bank of Canada forecast. This is news reporting and general information, not financial advice.*

## The 5 key signals

| # | Signal | Median expectation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Policy rate for the rest of 2026 | **Unchanged at 2.25%** |
| 2 | First increase | **2.50% in March 2027** |
| 3 | By Q3 2027 | **2.75%** |
| 4 | Risk skew | 40% see risks toward a **higher** path; 28% lower; 32% balanced |
| 5 | GDP growth to end-2026 | **1.3%**, revised down from 1.6% in Q1 |

## Reading the Bank of Canada market participants survey correctly

Two caveats matter before drawing conclusions, and both are easy to lose in a headline.

**The Bank of Canada market participants survey is a survey, not a forecast.** The Bank publishes what market professionals think; it does not endorse those views and is not bound by them. Roughly 26 respondents participate, which is a well-informed group but a small one.

**Respondents were genuinely divided.** A median path showing a 2027 hike sits alongside a risk split of 40% higher, 28% lower and 32% balanced. Nearly a third of participants saw the risks as evenly weighted, and more than a quarter still thought the greater danger was that rates end up *lower*. That is not consensus — it is a market that has stopped expecting cuts without becoming confident about hikes.

There is also a timing point. The survey was conducted **June 11 to 18**, which places it before the Bank's July 15 decision and before Statistics Canada reported that GDP rose 0.3% in May with an advance estimate of 0.8% growth for the second quarter. Firmer growth data since the survey closed points in the same direction as the median view rather than against it.

## Background: the rate path behind the Bank of Canada market participants survey

The Bank of Canada cut aggressively through the 2025 trade-related slowdown, ultimately reaching **2.25%**, and has now held there through six consecutive decisions — most recently on **July 15, 2026**. That keeps the commercial prime rate at **4.45%**.

Alongside the rate path, respondents trimmed their growth expectations: the median participant now expects Canadian GDP to be **1.3%** higher at the end of 2026 than a year earlier, down from **1.6%** in the first-quarter survey. That combination — slightly weaker growth, but no further easing — describes an economy the market believes is recovering without needing more help.

## Why this matters for borrowers

For two years, the reasonable planning assumption for Canadians with variable-rate debt was that payments would fall. The **Bank of Canada market participants survey** is the clearest indication yet that this assumption has expired.

What that means in practice:

- **Stop budgeting for cuts.** If your household plan assumes prime drops before 2027, revise it. The median market view is flat for the rest of this year.
- **Stress-test for a modest increase.** A 0.25 to 0.50 percentage point rise during 2027 is the central expectation. On a $300,000 variable-rate mortgage, half a point is roughly $75–$85 a month.
- **Fixed-versus-variable becomes a live question again.** When cuts were expected, variable carried an obvious advantage. On a flat-then-rising path, that advantage narrows.
- **Renewals in 2027 face a different backdrop.** Borrowers renewing next year should not count on lower rates arriving first.

One important limit. If your borrowing is short-term consumer credit rather than prime-linked debt, this changes almost nothing. Installment loans in Canada are capped at **35% APR** under the criminal interest rate provisions in force since January 1, 2025, and licensed payday loans at **$14 per $100**. Those ceilings are set in federal law and do not move with the policy rate — which is why the practical cost of high-cost credit is governed by the amount you borrow and the term you choose, not by the Bank of Canada.

## Key takeaways

- The **Bank of Canada market participants survey** for Q2 2026 shows the median respondent expecting **no change through 2026**, then **2.50% in March 2027** and **2.75% by Q3 2027**.
- The survey ran **June 11–18, 2026**, before the July decision and the May GDP release — both of which point the same way.
- The next expected move is **up**, reversing two years of easing expectations.
- Participants were split on risk: **40% higher, 28% lower, 32% balanced**.
- Median 2026 growth expectations were cut from **1.6% to 1.3%**.
- Prime remains **4.45%**; variable-rate borrowers should plan for flat, then modestly higher.
- Payday and installment loan pricing is capped by law and is unaffected.

## What to watch next

The Bank of Canada's Senior Loan Officer Survey is scheduled for **August 21, 2026** and will show whether lenders themselves are tightening or loosening credit conditions — a more direct read on approval odds than the rate path. The next scheduled policy decision will test whether the market's flat-for-2026 view holds.
