Canadians under 55 report worse outcomes than the national average on **every major financial measure** in the survey Equifax Canada released on **August 6, 2026** — and one pressure appears in their results that does not show up in the headline national numbers at all.

**In this article:** the age gap · the housing finding · what it isn't · the national context · why this matters · key takeaways · sources

## Canadians under 55: the gap, measure by measure

Equifax Canada surveyed **1,532 Canadians aged 18 to 65 between July 17 and 19, 2026**, using Leger's online panel, with a reported margin of error of **±2.5 per cent, 19 times out of 20**.

| Measure | All respondents | **Canadians under 55** |
|---|---|---|
| Using more credit for essentials | 29% | **36%** |
| Spending more than a year ago | 40% | **42%** |
| Expect only minimum credit card payments | 25% | **31%** |
| Expect to pay card balances in full | 56% | **47%** |
| **Struggling due to housing costs** | — | **22%** |

Every row moves the same direction. The largest gaps are on **credit for essentials** — seven percentage points — and on **paying balances in full**, where under-55s fall nine points below the national figure.

**Rebecca Oakes, Vice-President of Advanced Analytics at Equifax Canada**, said in the release that "the results point to mounting financial pressure for many households."

## The housing line

The finding with no national equivalent is the last row: **22 per cent of Canadians under 55 reported struggling because of housing costs.**

That matters because of what it cannot be substituted for. The survey shows households responding to pressure by trimming — **67 per cent** cut entertainment and leisure, **40 per cent** cut personal care, **32 per cent** reduced spending on essential living expenses including groceries and utilities.

Housing does not work that way. Rent and mortgage payments are fixed between renewals, they are the largest single line in most household budgets, and there is no cheaper substitute available in the same month. A household can buy less expensive groceries. It cannot buy a less expensive August.

That is the structural reason the under-55 numbers sit worse across the board: a larger share of their spending is committed before the month begins.

## What the gap is not

It is worth stating plainly what the data does not show, because generational financial stories routinely overreach.

**It is not a discipline gap.** Across all respondents, **60 per cent** said they are actively avoiding new debt, and two-thirds have cut discretionary spending. Nothing in the release attributes the under-55 gap to spending behaviour.

**It is not a majority in crisis.** **Forty-seven per cent** of under-55s still expect to pay their credit card balances in full each month. That is below the national 56 per cent, but it is close to half.

**It is not evenly distributed within the group.** The survey covers ages 18 to 65, so "under 55" spans people at very different life stages — early-career renters and mid-career households with mortgages and children are counted together.

## The national context around it

The under-55 figures sit inside a broader picture of households drawing down buffers:

- **23 per cent** of all respondents are drawing on savings to cover day-to-day costs
- **20 per cent** are relying more on credit cards or lines of credit
- **13 per cent** are borrowing money to cover basic living expenses
- **35 per cent** have reduced contributions to savings, investments or education funds
- **41 per cent** are concerned about being able to cover an emergency expense

Oakes described the compounding risk: **"When these pressures begin to overlap, households can lose financial flexibility quickly."**

For Canadians under 55 that overlap is more likely by construction — higher fixed housing costs leave less room to absorb everything else.

## Why this matters for Canadians under 55

Three implications for anyone in this group considering credit.

**Age is not an assessment factor, but its symptoms are.** No lender scores an applicant on being under 55. What lenders do read — utilisation, essentials appearing on credit, a savings balance trending down — are precisely the things this cohort reports more of.

**The emergency-expense figure is the one to act on.** Forty-one per cent of respondents cannot comfortably meet a surprise cost. That is the situation in which a manageable loan becomes a missed payment, because there is no reserve behind it. A modest pre-arranged buffer — an overdraft limit, an untouched credit line — costs little and is arranged on an ordinary day, not an urgent one.

**Housing costs deserve their own conversation.** For the 22 per cent struggling on housing specifically, borrowing addresses the symptom. Renewal timing, provincial rent rules and, where applicable, mortgage arrangements are where that pressure is actually resolved.

## Key takeaways

- **Canadians under 55** fare worse than the national average on every major survey measure.
- **36 per cent** use more credit for essentials (vs 29% nationally); **31 per cent** expect only minimum card payments (vs 25%).
- **47 per cent** expect to pay balances in full, against **56 per cent** nationally.
- **22 per cent** report struggling specifically because of **housing costs** — the pressure with no cheap substitute.
- Nationally, **60 per cent** are actively avoiding new debt — this is a cost squeeze, not a spending problem.
- Method: **1,532 Canadians aged 18–65**, **July 17–19, 2026**, Leger online panel, **±2.5 per cent, 19 times out of 20**.

## Conclusion

The consistent direction of the age gap is what makes it credible. A single outlying number could be noise; five measures all moving the same way, with a housing finding that explains the mechanism, is a pattern.

For Canadians under 55, the actionable part is narrower than the headline. Discretionary spending has largely already been cut. What remains is arranging a buffer before it is needed, and treating housing costs as a separate problem with separate remedies rather than something a consumer loan can solve.
