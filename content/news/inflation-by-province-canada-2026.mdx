Canada recorded 3.0% inflation in July 2026. Almost nobody actually experienced 3.0%. The **inflation by province Canada 2026** figures published by Statistics Canada on **August 17, 2026** show a spread of three full percentage points between the highest and lowest provinces — from **Nova Scotia at 5.0%** down to **Ontario at 2.0%**.

![Inflation by province Canada 2026 — comparing regional price increases against a household budget](https://images.pexels.com/photos/27742643/pexels-photo-27742643.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Summary

- National all-items CPI: **+3.0% y/y** in July 2026
- **Highest: Nova Scotia at 5.0%** — two points above the national rate
- **Lowest: Ontario at 2.0%** — unchanged from June, and exactly at the Bank of Canada's target
- Statistics Canada's regional notes cited **electricity and natural gas** among the drivers
- One national policy rate applies to every province regardless

A three-point gap between the extremes is the widest feature of the inflation by province Canada 2026 data. In practical terms, a household in Nova Scotia saw prices rise at more than double the rate faced by a household in Ontario over the same twelve months, on the same national basket.

## Why inflation by province Canada 2026 varies so much

The national CPI is a weighted average of provincial indexes, and several large components are set locally rather than nationally.

- **Electricity and natural gas.** Rates are set provincially, through regulators and utility applications, on schedules that differ by jurisdiction. Statistics Canada's regional highlights for July specifically referenced electricity and natural gas.
- **Provincial taxes and regulated charges.** Sales taxes and various levies differ, and changes take effect on provincial timetables.
- **Shelter.** Rent movements and housing costs vary substantially by local market, and rent-increase rules differ by province.
- **Fuel.** Provincial fuel taxes and regional supply conditions produce different pump prices for the same commodity.

Nationally, gasoline was the dominant story in July, up **25.7%** year over year against **20.5%** in June, with travel tours up **15.2%**. But the extent to which those national swings show up in any given province depends on local weightings and local tax treatment.

## Background: what the national number is for

The Bank of Canada targets **2% inflation** on the total national CPI, within a **control range of 1% to 3%**. July's 3.0% sits at the top edge of that range.

The Bank's preferred core measures held near target: **CPI-median 2.0%** and **CPI-trim 1.9%**, averaging **2.0%** against 1.9% in June. Excluding gasoline, national inflation was **2.2%**, unchanged for a third consecutive month.

Ontario's 2.0% reading is a striking coincidence — the country's largest provincial economy printed exactly the national target while the national headline printed the top of the range.

## Why this matters

Monetary policy takes no account of inflation by province Canada 2026 figures. It is national. There is one policy rate, and the next decision is scheduled for **September 2, 2026**. A borrower in Halifax and a borrower in Toronto pay interest set by the same committee, reading the same national aggregate — even though one of them has absorbed 5.0% price growth and the other 2.0%.

That has a concrete consequence. When inflation is concentrated regionally, national policy cannot target it, and it is not designed to. Provincial governments hold most of the levers that actually move a provincial CPI: utility regulation, energy rebates, sales taxes, rent rules.

For households, the useful conclusion is narrower and more practical. The national headline is a poor guide to your own cost of living. Inflation by province Canada 2026 data is closer, but even that averages across very different households — the true personal rate depends on how much you spend on fuel, utilities and rent relative to the basket.

If your budget has tightened noticeably more than the national number suggests, that is not a misperception. It may simply be where you live.

## Inflation by province Canada 2026: key takeaways

1. **A three-point spread separates the highest and lowest provinces** — Nova Scotia 5.0%, Ontario 2.0%.
2. **The national 3.0% describes almost no one exactly.**
3. **Electricity and natural gas** were named among the regional drivers.
4. **Ontario matched the 2% target exactly**, unchanged from June.
5. **One national policy rate applies regardless of provincial divergence**; the next decision is September 2, 2026.

## Conclusion

Averages conceal, and this month they conceal more than usual. Canada's July inflation rate was accurate as a national statistic and close to meaningless as a description of any individual household's experience.

The more useful exercise is to look at which categories drove your own costs — fuel, utilities, rent — and compare those against the provincial detail rather than the national headline.
